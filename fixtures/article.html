The History of Timekeeping

By A. Writer, Example Journal

Humans have measured time for millennia, beginning with the shadow cast by a stick in the ground. Sundials refined that shadow into hours, water clocks freed timekeeping from the sun, and mechanical escapements eventually made time portable.

The pendulum clock, introduced in the seventeenth century, improved accuracy from minutes to seconds per day. Marine chronometers then solved the longitude problem, transforming navigation and global trade.

Quartz oscillators displaced mechanical movements in the twentieth century, and atomic clocks now define the second itself, losing less than a second over millions of years. Modern life, from GPS to financial markets, quietly depends on that precision.
